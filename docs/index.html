Trust & limitations
  • No language model. Classification, ranking and dating are rule-based and reproducible.
  • No invented dates. Dates are read from the source; when none exists the item reads “date unknown” and is excluded from date-based figures.
  • No causal claims. We report what changed and how unusual it is — never why, beyond what the counts support.
  • Ranking is priority, not confidence. Order follows explicit additive rules; every item shows its own “Why ranked” breakdown.
  • Informational only — automated classification. Evidence stages, primary/secondary strength and market-access facets (decision and payer type) are generated by rules and a fixed taxonomy for triage — they are automated intelligence signals, not authoritative determinations, and may contain errors.
  • Verify against the source. An automated monitor can miss, misclassify or fail to date an item, and sources may change or retract. Nothing here is medical, regulatory, reimbursement, legal or investment advice, and inclusion of an item implies no endorsement.
  • Headlines, linked to their origin. We surface titles, links and short factual metadata with attribution — never full articles. All content remains © its original publisher; open the linked source to read and cite it.
Privacy & technical details
No accounts, tracking cookies or personal-data storage — your read/unread state stays in your browser only. The site is rebuilt daily from curated public APIs, feeds and official publications and served as a static site via GitHub Pages. An RSS feed of the top-ranked items is also available, and the current build can be downloaded as CSV or JSON. The engine is open source; the curated source list and ranking configuration are maintained privately.
Coverage & cadence
Coverage philosophy
Healthcare AI adoption depends on clinical evidence, regulatory clearance and payment pathways. We therefore prioritise primary sources — regulators, HTA agencies, trial registries and peer-reviewed literature — supplemented by established trade press where primary feeds are unavailable. Medtech and pharma vendor press releases are excluded to keep the feed independent; frontier-AI lab blogs are monitored only as an upstream capability signal.
Cadence
Rebuilt once each morning. Most updates are a day or two old; device authorisations reflect the FDA’s ~30-day publishing lag.
Frequently asked
How often does it update?Once each morning, rebuilt automatically. Most updates are a day or two old; FDA device authorisations reflect the agency’s ~30-day publishing lag.
Where do the items come from?Curated public sources — regulator, HTA and payer feeds, trial registries and peer-reviewed journals (primary sources wherever available), supplemented by selected industry publications where no primary feed exists. Ingested via public APIs, feeds, official publications and, where needed, tightly scoped news queries. The full list and exact queries are maintained privately.
How is each item classified?By transparent, deterministic rules based on source, terminology and lifecycle signals — no machine-learning model decides an item’s stage, region or body. Every ranking exposes its own “Why ranked” breakdown.
Why does an item show “date unknown”?Dates are read from the source. When a source exposes no usable date, the item is shown as “date unknown” rather than guessed, and it is excluded from any date-based figure.
Does it use AI to write or interpret the feed?No. No language model writes summaries, scores impact, or interprets any item. Classification, ranking, dating and every count come from transparent rules with no model, so the same inputs reproduce the same output.
A source I expected is missing — why?Coverage is deliberately curated for regulatory, clinical and reimbursement relevance, not volume, and medtech and pharma vendor press releases are excluded. Suggestions are welcome via the repository.
What this is
A daily monitor tracking how AI moves through healthcare — from research and clinical validation to regulation, HTA, reimbursement and adoption.
It combines public signals from regulators, HTA bodies, payer organisations, trial registries, journals and industry sources into one daily briefing — framed around two adoption questions: can it be sold? (authorisation) and will it be paid for? (coverage).
Scope: healthcare AI evidence, regulation, reimbursement and market signals — not a general AI news feed, investment advice or predictive analytics.
Who it’s for
Built for market-access, HEOR, regulatory and clinical teams who need evidence, regulatory and payment signals in one place, with every item traceable to its source. Investors tracking healthcare AI adoption may also find it useful.
How it stays credible
Classification and ranking are deterministic and rule-based. Dates come from the source or are shown as “date unknown.” No causal or predictive claims are made. Full methodology →
